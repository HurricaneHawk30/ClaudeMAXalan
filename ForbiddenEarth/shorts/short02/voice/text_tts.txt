In nineteen sixty-three, a man knocked down a wall in his basement and found a lost city.

He was renovating his house in [Derinkuyu](/dˌɛɹɪnkˈuju/), in central Turkey. Behind the wall was a room. Behind that room, another one. And then another. He had stumbled into an entire city, carved into the volcanic rock beneath his feet.

[Derinkuyu](/dˌɛɹɪnkˈuju/) goes down about eighty-five meters, with at least eighteen levels. It could shelter up to twenty thousand people, along with their animals. There are stables, wells, schools and chapels, all linked by tunnels. More than fifty air shafts kept the air fresh, and huge stone doors could seal the passages from the inside.
