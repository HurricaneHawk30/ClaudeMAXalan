This place is more acidic than battery acid. And it's on Earth.

[Dallol](/dɑlˈɑl/) lies in northeastern Ethiopia, about a hundred and twenty-five meters below sea level, in one of the hottest places on the planet. Deep below, magma heats water that rises through thick layers of salt. Where it breaks the surface, it builds bright yellow crusts, green and orange pools, and white salt chimneys, all hissing and bubbling.

NASA describes pools here with a P H of about zero point two five. Scientists come to ask one question: is there a limit to where life can survive? Some studies found tiny microbes. Others found nothing at all.
