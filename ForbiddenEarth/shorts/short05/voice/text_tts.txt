Near Madrid's Plaza [Mayor](/mɑjˈɔɹ/) stands [Sobrino](/sObɹˈinO/) de [Botín](/bOtˈin/). Guinness World Records lists it as the oldest restaurant in the world that is still open. A French cook named [Jean](/ʒˈɑn/) [Botín](/bOtˈin/) and his wife opened it as an inn in seventeen twenty-five. At first, it wasn't even allowed to sell meat or wine. Travelers brought their own food, and the kitchen cooked it for them.

The [Botíns](/bOtˈinz/) had no children, so the business passed to a nephew. That's why it's called [Sobrino](/sObɹˈinO/) de [Botín](/bOtˈin/): [Botín](/bOtˈin/)'s nephew.

Its wood-fired oven has been in use since the day it opened, and it still roasts the house dish, suckling pig.
