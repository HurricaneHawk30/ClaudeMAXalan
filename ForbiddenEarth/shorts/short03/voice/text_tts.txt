There's an island off Brazil where almost no one is allowed to land. Because of the snakes.

[Ilha](/ˈiljə/) [da](/dɑ/) [Queimada](/kAmˈɑdə/) [Grande](/ɡɹˈɑnʤi/) lies about thirty-five kilometers off the coast of [São](/sˈW/) [Paulo](/pˈWlO/) state. Brazilians call it Snake Island.

It's the only home of the golden [lancehead](/lˈænshˌɛd/), a pit viper found nowhere else on Earth. Cut off from the mainland for around eleven thousand years, the snakes learned to hunt birds. Their venom is said to be up to five times more potent than that of their mainland relatives. And there are a few thousand of them, on an island of just forty-three hectares.

Only the navy and a handful of vetted scientists may land.
