 108.6 The Hall of Records, USA.
 111.8 Behind the faces of Mount Rushmore in a small canyon,
 115.6 there's a tunnel most visitors never see.
 119.3 The sculptor Gutsen Borglum dreamed of a great hall of records carved into the mountain,
 124.7 reached by a grand staircase, where people in the far future could learn who these four presidents were and why the monument was built.
 133.0 In the late 1930s, workers blasted a tunnel about 20 meters into the granite.
 138.4 Then, the work stopped, Borglum died, and the hall was left unfinished.
 145.3 In 1998, almost sixty years later, his family and the National Park Service finished the job in a smaller way.
 153.8 Sixteen porcelain enamel panels, carrying the Declaration of Independence, the Constitution,

 179.1 Number 8.
 179.9 The Svalbard Global Seed Vault, Norway.
 183.5 On a frozen island halfway between mainland Norway and the North Pole,
 188.2 a narrow concrete wedge sticks out of a mountainside.
 192.0 Behind its doors, a tunnel leads deep into the rock to vaults kept at minus 18 degrees Celsius.
 200.9 Inside are more than a million seed samples from almost every country on Earth.
 206.6 Wheat, rice, barley, beans, thousands of varieties of the plants humanity eats.
 213.3 It's a backup in case war or disaster destroys the seed banks we depend on.
 220.2 The vault opens only a few times a year.
 223.4 The first real withdrawal came during the war in Syria.
 227.2 A seed bank near Aleppo was cut off, and its scientists took their backup seeds out of Svalbard to rebuild their collection.
 235.6 In 2017, even this fortress had a scare.
 240.3 Unusually, warm weather melted the permafrost and water flowed into the entrance tunnel.
 246.1 The seeds inside stayed frozen and safe.
 251.0 Number 7.
 252.1 Movile Cave, Romania In 1986, workers in Romania were drilling near the Black Sea coast,
 260.7 looking for a place to build a power plant.
 263.6 Instead, they broke into a cave that had been cut off from the outside world for millions of years.
 271.2 Scientists estimate that Movile cave was sealed off about five and a half million years ago.
 277.3 No sunlight has ever reached it.
 279.7 The air holds about half the normal amount of oxygen and is thick with carbon dioxide and hydrogen sulfide,
 287.4 a gas that smells like rotten eggs and can poison a person.
 292.8 And yet the cave is full of life.
 295.6 Spiders, water scorpions, leeches, centipedes, almost 50 species so far,
 301.9 and most of them live nowhere else on Earth.
 304.7 Their food chain doesn't start with sunlight.
 307.6 It starts with bacteria that feed on chemicals in the water.
 312.2 Fewer than a hundred people have ever been allowed inside.

 330.6 They dug through, climbed down, and found walls covered in paintings—horses,
 335.8 lions, rhinos, mammoths, and bears.
 340.7 The paintings are around 36 ,000 years old, about twice as old as the famous art of Lascaux.

 378.1 The Runit Dome, Marshall Islands.
 381.4 On a narrow island in the middle of the Pacific,

 391.3 Between 1946 and 1958, the United States tested dozens of nuclear bombs in the Marshall Islands.
 399.4 One of them, in 1958, left a crater on Runit Island.

 419.7 The dome was never meant to last forever.
 422.4 The crater under it was never lined, so seawater moves in and out underneath.
 427.9 Cracks have appeared on its surface.

 447.5 Number 4.
 448.5 Lake Vostok, Antarctica.
 451.4 Under the ice of Antarctica, almost 4 kilometers down, lies a lake about the size of Lake Ontario.
 459.3 Lake Vostok has been sealed beneath the ice sheet for an estimated 15 million years,
 465.0 in total darkness, under enormous pressure.
 468.9 It stays liquid because heat rising from the Earth below and the thick ice above keep it from freezing.
 476.7 If anything lives down there, it has been cut off from the rest of life on Earth for millions of years.
 483.9 Russian scientists spent more than 20 years drilling down from Vostok Station,
 489.3 one of the coldest places on the planet.
 491.3 In February 2012, their drill finally touched the water at a depth of about 3 ,770 meters.
 501.1 The lake water rushed up the borehole and froze.
 505.5 Some scientists worry that the kerosene used in the drilling could have contaminated the samples.
 511.7 What really lives in Lake Vostok is still an open question.

 654.5 sealed under the rock, waiting.
