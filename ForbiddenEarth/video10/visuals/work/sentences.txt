  30.8 Skeleg Michael, Ireland About 12 km off the coast of Ireland,
  36.9 a jagged peak of rock rises straight out of the Atlantic.
  41.1 More than a thousand years ago, a small group of monks climbed it and decided to live there,
  46.9 as far from the world as they could get.
  50.2 They cut 618 stone steps into the cliffs and built round huts of stacked stone without a drop of mortar.
  58.6 Those huts are still standing.
  60.7 For centuries around 12 monks and an abbot lived here until they finally moved to the mainland.
  68.6 Today Skelligmichael is a world heritage site and nobody lives on it.
  73.8 Boats can land only in summer and only when the sea allows.
  78.5 Visitors are counted and nobody is allowed to stay the night.
  82.2 The only ones who stay are the puffins.

  87.8 Henderson Island, Pitcairn Islands In the middle of the South Pacific,
  93.1 thousands of kilometers from any city, lies a raised coral island covered in thick jungle.
  99.8 Polynesians once lived here, but for at least 600 years,
 104.0 Henderson Island has had no people at all.
 107.2 It's a world heritage site, protected for its untouched wildlife,
 111.9 and it's visited only once every few years by scientists.
 116.9 When researchers came in 2015, they found something they didn't expect.
 121.5 The beaches were covered in plastic, bottles, fishing nets, helmets,
 126.4 food containers.
 127.8 They estimated nearly 38 million pieces, weighing more than 17 tons,
 133.5 with thousands of new pieces washing up every day.
 138.0 None of it came from the island.
 139.5 Ocean currents carry it here from all over the world.
 143.8 Nobody lives on Henderson Island, but everybody's trash does.

 150.7 Balls Pyramid, Australia 20 kilometers from Lord Howe Island, a blade of volcanic rock rises 562 meters out of the Tasman Sea.
 161.7 It's the tallest sea stack in the world.
 165.3 There's no beach, no harbor, and almost nowhere to stand.
 170.8 On nearby Lord Howe Island lived a giant stick insect as long as a human hand.
 177.0 Locals called it the tree lobster.
 179.3 In 1918, a supply ship ran aground there, rats escaped onto the island,
 185.2 and within a few years, the insect was gone.
 189.0 Scientists declared it extinct.
 191.7 Then in 2001, a team climbed Ball's pyramid at night.
 197.0 On a bare cliff under a single shrub, they found 24 of them still alive.
 203.6 Climbing the pyramid was banned in 1982, and landing now needs special permission.
 210.3 The last wild tree lobsters on Earth still live on that rock.
 216.6 7.
 217.2 Povellia, Italy From the beaches of Venice, you can see a small island with an old bell tower and empty buildings.
 225.8 That's Povellia.
 227.4 Nobody has lived there since 1968.
 231.7 From 1776, Poveglia was a quarantine station.
 236.4 Ships arriving in Venice had to stop here, and sailors who might carry the plague were held on the island.
 242.8 In the 20th century, it became a hospital.
 246.3 When the hospital closed, the island was left to the trees.
 251.7 Legends say tens of thousands of plague victims are buried here.
 256.3 Historians say the real number is far smaller, but the story stuck,
 261.5 and Poveglia became known as one of the most haunted places in the world.
 266.8 The island is still closed to the public.
 269.6 In 2014, the Italian government put it up for auction.
 274.7 Thousands of locals chipped in to stop it.
 277.1 Now they plan to turn part of it into a public park.

 283.0 The Farallon Islands, USA.
 286.0 About 45 kilometers west of the Golden Gate Bridge, a ring of sharp granite islands rises out of the Pacific.
 294.9 Technically, they're part of the city of San Francisco, but since 1969,
 300.9 the public hasn't been allowed to set foot on them.
 304.9 The only people who live here are a few researchers.
 307.6 They share the islands with hundreds of thousands of seabirds and with seals and sea lions.
 314.9 Every autumn, great white sharks gather around the islands to hunt them.
 320.4 There's a darker secret under the waves.
 323.3 From 1946 to 1970, nearly 48 ,000 barrels of low -level radioactive waste were dumped on the seafloor around the islands.
 333.0 Scientists have mapped many of them.
 336.3 They're still down there.

 340.6 Heard Island, Australia Near Antarctica in the stormy southern ocean lies one of the most remote places on Earth.
 349.6 It takes about two weeks by ship from Perth to get here.
 353.3 Heard Island has glaciers, black sand beaches, and an active volcano called Big Ben,
 359.4 whose summit is the highest point in all of Australia.
 364.6 No one lives on Heard Island.
 366.8 There are no buildings and no roads.
 369.2 Even scientists need a permit to land.
 372.1 The only residents are penguins, seals, and seabirds.
 377.1 And yet in April 2025, Heard Island and the nearby McDonald Islands appeared on a list of places hit by new United States tariffs,
 386.7 10 % on goods from an island with no people,
 390.2 no shops, and no factories.
 392.6 The internet joked about tariffs on penguins.
 395.9 The penguins didn't comment.
 399.4 Number 4.
 400.3 Bouvet Island, Norway.
 403.0 Bouvet Island is the most remote island on Earth.
 406.0 The nearest land, the coast of Antarctica, is about 1 ,700 kilometers away,
 412.5 and nobody lives there either.
 414.8 Almost the entire island is covered by a glacier sitting on top of a dead volcano.
 421.8 It was discovered in 1739, but it was so hard to find again that the famous explorer James Cook went looking for it and gave up.
 432.6 In 1964, a team landed by helicopter and found something that shouldn't have been there.
 438.9 In a small lagoon lay an abandoned lifeboat.
 442.6 Nearby were oars and supplies.
 445.4 There was no name on the boat and no sign of the people who came in it.
 450.4 No one knows who they were or what happened to them.
 454.0 Today, Bouvet is a nature reserve, and almost no one is allowed to land at all.

 462.3 Clipperton Island, France In the eastern Pacific, more than a thousand kilometers from Mexico,
 469.8 there's a ring of coral around a stagnant lagoon.
 473.5 It's named after an English pirate who is said to have used it as a hideout.
 479.2 In the early 1900s, Mexico sent about a hundred people here,
 484.2 soldiers and their families, to mine bird droppings for fertilizer and keep a lighthouse.
 490.2 A supply ship came every couple of months.
 493.3 Then, during the Mexican Revolution, the ship stopped coming.
 498.5 People died of hunger and scurvy.
 500.9 The governor drowned trying to reach a passing ship.
 503.5 The last man left on the island, the lighthouse keeper,
 507.6 declared himself king and terrorized the women and children.
 511.8 In July 1917, the women fought back and killed him.
 516.3 The very next day, an American warship arrived and rescued 11 survivors.
 523.1 Clipperton now belongs to France.
 525.6 Apart from short military and scientific visits, nobody has lived there since.

 533.8 Vazryzdeniya Island, Central Asia Its name means Rebirth Island.
 540.2 It lay in the middle of the Aral Sea, once one of the largest lakes in the world between what are now Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan.
 548.4 For decades, it was a secret.
 552.0 From the 1950s, the Soviet Union used the island as a testing ground for biological weapons.
 558.0 a closed town was built for the scientists and soldiers.
 562.7 In 1992, after the Soviet Union collapsed, everyone left almost overnight.
 569.0 Years earlier, tons of anthrax had been buried in pits on the island.
 573.9 In 2002, an American and Uzbek team came to clean it up.
 580.2 Meanwhile, the Aral Sea was dying, drained to water cotton fields.
 585.5 The water around the island dried up and in 2001 Rebirth Island stopped being an island at all.

 595.4 North Sentinel Island, India In the Bay of Bengal, among the Andaman Islands,
 601.8 lies an island about the size of Manhattan, ringed by coral reefs and covered in forest.
 608.7 It is home to the Sentinelese, one of the last peoples on earth who have never accepted contact with the outside world.
 617.7 Indian law forbids anyone to visit, or even to come within about nine kilometers of the shore.
 624.5 One reason is disease.
 626.7 The Sentinelese have no immunity to common illnesses, and an ordinary flu could be deadly for them.
 634.3 They have made their wishes clear.
 636.9 After the 2000 and 4 tsunami, a helicopter flew over to check on them,
 642.1 and a man on the beach aimed an arrow at it.
 645.1 Outsiders who came too close have been killed, most recently an American missionary in 2018.
 652.1 As recently as 2025, a tourist who stepped onto the beach was arrested.
 658.3 So the island stays exactly as it has for thousands of years.
 663.2 The kindest thing anyone can do is leave it alone.
