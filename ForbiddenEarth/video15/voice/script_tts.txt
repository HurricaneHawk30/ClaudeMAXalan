=== 00_hook ===
Most volcanoes sleep for thousands of years. These ten don't. One of them dropped ash on one of the biggest cities in the world this year. One drained a lake of lava in less than an hour. One split a town in two and sent all its people away in a single night. And at number one: a volcano so wide and so flat that half a million people live inside it, and the ground under their homes is rising right now.

=== 01_fuji ===
Number ten. Mount [Fuji](/fˈuʤi/), Japan.

It's the most famous mountain in Japan, an almost perfect cone, three thousand seven hundred and seventy-six meters tall. On a clear day, you can see it from Tokyo, about a hundred kilometers away. It's easy to forget that it's an active volcano.

[Fuji](/fˈuʤi/) last erupted in seventeen oh seven, just forty-nine days after one of the strongest earthquakes in Japan's history. For about sixteen days it threw ash into the sky, and that ash fell on [Edo](/ˈɛdO/), the city we now call Tokyo.

Today, more than thirty-seven million people live in the Tokyo region. In twenty twenty-five, Japan's government issued new guidelines for a [Fuji](/fˈuʤi/) eruption, warning that ash could stop trains, cut power and block roads across the capital within days. Planners estimate that an eruption like the one in seventeen oh seven could leave around four hundred and ninety million cubic meters of ash across the region. Before seventeen oh seven, [Fuji](/fˈuʤi/) erupted far more often. Scientists say its long silence doesn't mean it's finished.

=== 02_rainier ===
Number nine. Mount [Rainier](/ɹAnˈɪɹ/), USA.

Mount [Rainier](/ɹAnˈɪɹ/) towers over the Seattle area at four thousand three hundred and ninety-two meters, and it carries more glacier ice than any other peak in the lower forty-eight states. That ice is the problem.

When a volcano like this wakes up, it can melt its glaciers fast. And its weak, rotten rock can simply collapse. Either way, the result is a [lahar](/lˈɑhɑɹ/): a river of mud, rock and broken trees that can travel tens of kilometers down a valley in about an hour. Around five hundred years ago, one of these, the Electron Mudflow, reached the lowlands where towns like [Orting](/ˈɔɹtɪŋ/) stand today.

In twenty eighteen, the U.S. Geological Survey ranked [Rainier](/ɹAnˈɪɹ/) the third most dangerous volcano in the country. Around eighty thousand people live in areas that [lahars](/lˈɑhɑɹz/) could reach. Since nineteen ninety-eight, sensors have been watching the valleys. In the towns below, warning sirens are tested, and schoolchildren practice walking to high ground. In some places, they'd have less than an hour.

=== 03_taal ===
Number eight. [Taal](/tɑˈɑl/) Volcano, the Philippines.

[Taal](/tɑˈɑl/) is small. Its island rises only about three hundred meters out of a lake on the island of [Luzon](/luzˈɑn/), around sixty kilometers south of Manila. But it's one of the most active volcanoes in the Philippines, and one of the deadliest.

In nineteen eleven, an eruption here killed more than thirteen hundred people. In January twenty twenty, [Taal](/tɑˈɑl/) woke up again, blasting ash more than fifteen kilometers into the sky. Lightning flashed inside the cloud, ash fell on Manila, and hundreds of thousands of people had to leave their homes.

The island itself is now a Permanent Danger Zone, where nobody is allowed to live. But [Taal](/tɑˈɑl/) hasn't calmed down. It had a string of small eruptions through twenty twenty-six, and in October it sent up another column of ash about four kilometers high. And all around the lake lies one of the most crowded regions of the country.

=== 04_etna ===
Number seven. Mount [Etna](/ˈɛtnə/), Italy.

[Etna](/ˈɛtnə/) is Europe's most active volcano, rising more than three thousand meters above the island of Sicily. It erupts so often that in [Catania](/kətˈɑnjə/), the city at its foot, people sweep black ash off their balconies the way other people clear snow.

In August twenty twenty-six, [Etna](/ˈɛtnə/) erupted from one of its summit craters, the [Voragine](/vOɹˈɑʤinA/). Ash and fountains of lava forced [Catania's](/kətˈɑnjəz/) airport to close again and again for about a week. Around seven hundred flights were cancelled, and around a hundred thousand travelers were stranded in the middle of the summer holidays. The ash cloud drifted as far as Malta and Libya.

Then, at the end of September, [Etna](/ˈɛtnə/) erupted again. It almost never stays quiet for long. Scientists watch it every hour of every day, because [Catania](/kətˈɑnjə/) and dozens of towns are built right on its slopes.

=== 05_nyiragongo ===
Number six. Mount [Nyiragongo](/nˌɪɹəɡˈɔŋɡO/), Democratic Republic of the Congo.

For decades, [Nyiragongo](/nˌɪɹəɡˈɔŋɡO/) held one of the largest lava lakes on Earth, a boiling pool of molten rock inside its crater. And its lava is unusual. It's so runny that it moves almost like water.

In nineteen seventy-seven, the crater walls cracked, and the lava lake drained in less than an hour. The lava raced down the slopes at up to sixty kilometers an hour, the fastest lava flow ever recorded. In two thousand two, lava poured straight into the city of [Goma](/ɡˈOmə/), through its streets and across part of its airport runway. And in May twenty twenty-one, it erupted again with almost no warning. The lava stopped just short of the city, but at least thirty-two people died, and hundreds of thousands fled.

[Goma](/ɡˈOmə/), home to more than a million people, lies only about fifteen kilometers from the crater. Every eruption here is a race against time.

=== 06_sakurajima ===
Number five. [Sakurajima](/sˌɑkuɹəʤˈimə/), Japan.

Across the bay from the city of [Kagoshima](/kˌɑɡOʃˈimə/), home to about half a million people, [Sakurajima](/sˌɑkuɹəʤˈimə/) erupts all the time. Small explosions happen many times a year, sometimes hundreds of times, throwing ash high over the water.

People here live with it. The city hands out special bags for collecting volcanic ash. Weather reports tell you which way the ash will blow. And on the volcano itself, children wear helmets on their way to school.

But it hasn't always been small explosions. In nineteen fourteen, [Sakurajima](/sˌɑkuɹəʤˈimə/) had the biggest eruption in twentieth-century Japan. So much lava poured out that the island became joined to the mainland. Fifty-eight people died. Today, the volcano is on alert level three, which means: do not approach. And scientists who measure the ground say magma has been building up under the bay again for more than a century.

=== 07_reykjanes ===
Number four. The [Reykjanes](/ɹˈAkjənˌɛs/) Peninsula, Iceland.

For about eight hundred years, the volcanoes of southwest Iceland were quiet. Then, in twenty twenty-one, they woke up. And in November twenty twenty-three, the ground under the fishing town of [Grindavík](/ɡɹˈɪndəvˌik/) split open. Hundreds of earthquakes shook the town, streets cracked in two, and all of its people, around thirty-seven hundred of them, were evacuated in a single night.

Since then, the crater row next to [Grindavík](/ɡɹˈɪndəvˌik/) has erupted again and again, nine times between December twenty twenty-three and the summer of twenty twenty-five. In January twenty twenty-four, lava crossed the town's defenses and burned several homes. The famous Blue Lagoon had to be evacuated many times. Iceland built huge earth walls to steer the lava away from the town and from the [Svartsengi](/svˈɑɹtsɛnɡi/) power plant.

Then the eruptions paused. But underground, magma has kept flowing back into the chamber beneath [Svartsengi](/svˈɑɹtsɛnɡi/). Iceland's Met Office says another eruption is likely, and it could start with less than an hour's warning. Most of [Grindavík's](/ɡɹˈɪndəvˌiks/) people still haven't moved back home.

=== 08_merapi ===
Number three. Mount [Merapi](/məɹˈɑpi/), Indonesia.

Its name means "Mountain of Fire," and it lives up to it. [Merapi](/məɹˈɑpi/), on the island of Java, is the most active volcano in Indonesia, with small eruptions every few years. Millions of people live within sight of it, including the city of [Yogyakarta](/jˌɑɡjəkˈɑɹtə/), less than thirty kilometers away.

[Merapi's](/məɹˈɑpiz/) deadliest weapon is the [pyroclastic](/pˌIɹOklˈæstɪk/) flow: a glowing avalanche of hot gas, ash and rock that can rush down its slopes at well over a hundred kilometers an hour. Nothing outruns it.

In twenty ten, [Merapi](/məɹˈɑpi/) had its biggest eruption in more than a century. Pyroclastic flows swept down the valleys, more than three hundred and fifty people died, and around three hundred and fifty thousand were evacuated. Since November twenty twenty, the volcano has been on alert level three out of four. A lava dome keeps growing at its summit, and hot avalanches keep rolling down its slopes.

=== 09_popocatepetl ===
Number two. [Popocatépetl](/pˌOpOkətˈɛpətəl/), Mexico.

In the old Aztec language, its name means "Smoking Mountain." Mexicans just call it "[El Popo](/ɛl pˈOpO/)." At more than five thousand four hundred meters, it's the second-highest peak in Mexico, and it stands about seventy kilometers from Mexico City. Around twenty-five million people live within a hundred kilometers of its crater.

After decades of quiet, [Popocatépetl](/pˌOpOkətˈɛpətəl/) woke up in nineteen ninety-four, and it has been erupting on and off ever since, puffing out steam, gas and ash almost every day. In twenty twenty-three, falling ash shut down the airports of Mexico City and [Puebla](/pwˈɛblə/) for hours, and schools around the volcano were closed. In February twenty twenty-six, ash fell on Mexico City again.

The alert level is yellow, and nobody is allowed within twelve kilometers of the crater. Scientists watch it around the clock, ready to call for evacuations if it turns from noisy to violent.

=== 10_campi_flegrei ===
Number one. [Campi Flegrei](/kˈɑmpi flɛɡɹˈA/), Italy.

You probably know Vesuvius, the volcano that buried Pompeii. But just west of Naples, there is another volcano, much bigger and much harder to see. [Campi Flegrei](/kˈɑmpi flɛɡɹˈA/), the "Burning Fields," is not a mountain. It's a [caldera](/kældˈɛɹə/), a giant collapsed crater about fifteen kilometers wide, and part of the Naples area is built right inside it.

About thirty-nine thousand years ago, it produced the largest eruption in Europe in the last two hundred thousand years. Its last eruption was much smaller. In fifteen thirty-eight, after years of earthquakes and rising ground, the earth opened near the town of [Pozzuoli](/pɑtswˈOli/) and built a brand-new hill in about a week. It's still there. It's called [Monte Nuovo](/mˈɑntA nwˈOvO/), "the New Mountain."

Now the ground is rising again. Since two thousand five, the center of [Pozzuoli](/pɑtswˈOli/) has risen by nearly one and a half meters, recently by about a centimeter a month. Earthquakes shake the area more and more often, and on July thirty-first, twenty twenty-six, the strongest one since modern monitoring began hit with a magnitude of four point seven. About half a million people live in the official red zone, the area that would have to be evacuated first.

Scientists stress that rising ground doesn't mean an eruption is certain, or coming soon. But [Campi Flegrei](/kˈɑmpi flɛɡɹˈA/) is the volcano that experts watch most closely in Europe. Because if it ever does erupt, it won't be on a mountain far away. It will be under a city.
